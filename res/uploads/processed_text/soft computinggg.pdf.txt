ABSTRACT: 
 
The huge number of publications in the few years has made it really hard to do 
a good job of looking at the literature for many subjects. This is because it 
takes a lot of time to find and look at all the articles for a study. Using 
keywords to search is not very helpful either. This is because it does not tell the 
user what the people doing the study were trying to find out. It also does not 
say what methods they used to do their research and what areas they were 
looking at. The literature, for disciplines is just too much to handle. Doing an 
analysis is hard when you have to look at so many publications. The people 
doing the study used methods and looked at different areas but you cannot tell 
that from just using keywords. The goal of this proposed project is to develop a 
tool that will enable scholars and researchers to conduct a thorough literature 
evaluation through the use of semantic embeddings, which provides a more 
accurate means of finding and understanding articles that relate to their 
research area. 
 
This thing uses ideas in big language models and Natural Language Processing 
methods to give smart analysis of research papers that makes sense. It collects 
research materials. Gets them ready by uploading documents before pulling out 
the text. The text it collects is broken down into parts so it can handle a lot of 
documents without losing the meaning of what the research papers are saying. 
Research papers are looked at carefully by this method to understand what they 
are, about. Each section of text is then represented in an electronic format as 
semantic embedding, where the meaning is inferred rather than by the use of 
keyword matching. 
 
Using a method called semantic similarity search, we create an embedded query 
from our user's details and find it in a document embedding database. We then 
send any associated research data back to our large language model to generate 
related, coherent responses and provide context through research discoveries, 
comparative studies, etc., while minimizing the chances of producing false 
results.
INTRODUCTION: 
 
 
Finding, reviewing, and summarizing academic literature has altered significantly 
as a result of the rise of digital research archives. Researchers employ digital 
technology to assess the literature, identify research gaps, and perform 
comparative analyses as a result of the increasing number of publications 
published in journals, conferences, and preprint platforms. Accurate, 
dependable, and scalable automated systems for literature analysis are 
desperately needed as the body of scholarly literature and multidisciplinary 
research expands and changes. Given their impact on scholarly output and 
research direction, it is imperative that the automated search recommendations 
are reliable, factual, and contextually relevant. 
 
Recent advances in artificial intelligence, especially with regard to massive 
language models, present a chance to construct interactive systems that could 
function as research assistants, gathering data from publications and responding 
to inquiries about scholarly topics. In contrast to other formal academic 
resources, probabilistic generation is used in less restricted models of big 
language systems, which can result in inaccurate summary statements, 
inaccurate information, and cyclical references. 
 
[1]By using research documents, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) 
approaches have been effective in reducing hallucinations and improving the 
factual accuracy of LLM-generated responses. 
 
[2] Several research studies have been carried out on the application in an effort 
to expand on the significant efforts to improve the degree of relevance and 
personalization. 
 
[3] 
Other research assess the reliability of theoretical systems based on LLM 
and RAG using each of the post-generation evaluation methodologies. In 
contrast to the architectural strategies utilized in response production control, 
the techniques focus on evaluation. 
 
[4] According to the research on knowledge-intensive document analysis, RAG-
based systems are more accurate and faithful to the source than LLMs; 
nonetheless, retrieval is not seen to be enough on its own to prevent 
unsupported output. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
[5] Controlled generation techniques are essential in both scientific and decision 
support systems since their goal is to produce condition response generation 
while ensuring that there is enough data recovered that meet this requirement 
and to have internal mechanisms available to allow for dependable output. 
 
 
Based on this information, when writing a literature review it is required that the 
review contain controlled generation techniques such as (i) how much RAG has 
been able to add to grounding but has not done so at a level significant to 
support over 50% of conditioned responses; and (ii) LLMs that operate alone 
(stand alone) tend to be unreliable for providing determinations regarding 
research papers. In response to these findings, I have completed the RPA 
assistant (a paper analysis and literature review assistant that uses semantic 
embedding) in accordance with the above stated requirements
MOTIVATION: 
A literature review usually takes a time. This is because many scientific articles 
are being published every year. When you do research it can be really hard to 
find the literature in journals that publish a lot of things. The problem is that 
computers are not good at understanding what research studies really mean. 
This is because the meaning of research is very complicated and you cannot 
figure it out by looking at the words that are used in the study. A literature 
review is still a job and it is hard to find the right literature. Computers are just 
not able to understand the meaning of research studies, like a literature 
review. However, by using semantic embeddings, researchers can compare 
scientific studies using the meaning of the studies instead of comparing them 
based only on the specific words used to create the study. LLM technology 
allows researchers to summarize, compare, and evaluate the quality of 
research articles, which will help them be more creative and efficient with their 
time. 
                
CHALLENGES: 
The size of papers is really big and they are getting more complicated. This 
means that scientific papers are too long for Large Language Models. To make 
embeddings for a lot of articles you need a lot of computer power and storage 
space. It is very hard to find the parts of the paper and if the paper is not broken 
up correctly you might lose the context that is necessary for understanding the 
scientific papers. Scientific papers have a lot of information. This information is 
critical, for understanding the scientific papers. As a result, if there is 
missing/contextual information an LLM could produce a gibberish/no 
reasonable summary of that document, thus rendering it useless for use within 
educational settings.
